All about wolfs!

Basic info

Wolves, also known as the gray wolf, are carnivorous mammals that mainly feed off of deer, elk and other herbivores.

These fluffy creatures are often compared to canines, because they are ancestors of domestic dogs!

Wolves can be found in forests, grasslands, tundra, and even deserts due to their high adaptability.

==========================================

Behaviour and Lifestyle

Wolves are social animals that live in family groups called packs, usually lead by a leader.

A pack communicates in order to survive, involving vocalizations like howls, as well as scent marking.

Wolves have very dense and fluffy winter fur, with a short undercoat and long, coarse guard hairs. Most of the undercoat and some guard hairs are shed in spring and grow back in autumn.

==========================================

Culture and History

Throughout history, wolves have shown up in myths, folklore, religion, and mythology as symbols of strenght, and sometimes danger.

The thing that contributed the most to their bad reputation were tales like "Little Red Riding Hood", "The Three Little Pigs" and "The Wolf and the Seven Young Goats", portraying the wolf as vilanious and malicious.

Wolfs are a common motif in mythology and cosmology with their association with Gods, like Apollo and Mars, by The Ancient Greek and The Ancient Romans.

==========================================

Fun facts!

  1. Despite seeming scary at first, wolves are playful among themselves.
  2. Wolves can run up to about 50-60 km/h, making them great hunters!
  3. The global wolf population was estimated to be 300 thousand in 2003.
  4. Through gray wolves, there have been recognized over 30 subspecies.
